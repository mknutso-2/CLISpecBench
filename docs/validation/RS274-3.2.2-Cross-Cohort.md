# RS274 v3.2.2 cross-cohort regrade check

An independent follow-up to [RS274-3.2.2.md](RS274-3.2.2.md), carried out by two agents working
against commit `9425111` and recording their exchange in an append-only log. It changes no test,
prompt, reference implementation, or published result.

## Why it was needed

The v3.2.1/v3.2.2 fixture corrections were derived by analyzing the GPT-6 Astra cohort's failures:
133 preliminary failed outcomes, 108 of them traced to five shared setup assumptions. That is a
sound way to find fixture bugs, and it is also the process most likely to produce a suite fitted to
one model. The audit's controls were internal to that cohort.

This check regrades a different cohort under the corrected rubric: the 36 published `api-only`
GPT-5.6 RS274 runs at eval 3.2.0 that have preserved source and prompt hashes byte-identical to the
Astra runs.

**This is not a blinded holdout, and it must not be described as one.** `Evals/RS274/TODO.md`, as
of `9938b1b` and therefore before the audit commits, already records two of these runs by name —
GPT-5.6 Terra high and max C++ — together with their official scores (261/546, 266/546) and
diagnostic scores of 442/546 and 484/546 obtained from manually modified copies of those
submissions with only their EOF rejection disabled, and `RS274-3.2.1.md` refers to that TODO. That
is source-mutant evidence, distinct in kind from the unchanged-source regrades reported here.
Those runs were known and used investigatively while the corrections were being considered.
Nothing here shows fixtures were tuned to them, but the cohort cannot serve as proof of
independence from all design influence. What it does establish is **applicability beyond Astra**.

### A withdrawn interpretation

An intermediate draft proposed that the twenty TLC/probe failures were scored against the
explicit-Z/TLC ambiguity that RS274-3.2.1.md defers to a public second pass. **That was wrong and
is withdrawn.** Section 2.1.2.10 "Current Position" requires that position numbers "must be
adjusted in the absence of any axis motion" when "Tool length offset is changed", and the worked
example in `RS274NGC.md` around lines 3938–3949 shows `g43 h2` emitting
`USE_TOOL_LENGTH_OFFSET(1.0000)` with no motion, after which a move with no Z word emits Z −0.5.
The deferred question concerns an *explicit* Z command issued while compensation is already active,
which none of the failing cases exercise. The twenty failures are genuine defects.

All 36 runs were regraded under one grader image, selected by published run UID rather than by
score. Full table and per-record provenance:

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The corrections apply beyond Astra | 6 of 36 GPT-5.6 submissions reach Astra's regraded band (≥549/555); C++ sol-max reaches 555/555 |
| The corrected rubric is not uniformly generous | 4 submissions score lower than under 3.2.0 |
| Exact ordering changes, concentrated in C++ | Spearman 0.899 over 36; per language C++ 0.667, JS 0.950, Rust 0.967, Python 0.996 |

### The largest regression is a real defect, and the mechanism is percent handling

C++ luna-high falls from 386/546 to 39/555. This is not an environment artifact: the C++, Python
and JavaScript reference implementations were graded under the same image and reproduced their
documented figures exactly (447/555, 555/555, 447/555).

The mechanism was established by compiling that submission's unchanged `main.cpp` in the same
offline image and running paired probes. Its input loop breaks on **any** standalone `%` line,
including the opening delimiter, before executing a block:

```cpp
if(raw.find('%')!=string::npos&&noSpace(raw)=="%")break;
```

So unframed `G0 X10\nG0 X20` ends at X=20, framed `%\nG0 X10\nG0 X20\n%` ends at X=0, and
closing-percent-only `G0 X10\nG0 X20\n%` ends at X=20. Under framed programs the submission
executes nothing and reports startup defaults. An earlier draft of this document proposed that it
dropped only the *final* block before the closing `%`; that was inferred from two-line fixtures
whose defaults coincide with the first block's effect (`G17\nG18` reporting G17, `G90\nG91`
reporting G90, `G94\nG93` reporting G94), and it is withdrawn.

The file-demarcation requirement is in `RS274NGC.md` **section 3.2**, the same section
`Evals/RS274/TODO.md` proposes to override for plain-EOF termination.

This is the mirror image of the audit's own correction. Framing the test programs both stopped
penalizing submissions that correctly require delimiters and started penalizing one that mishandles
them. It is evidence against reading v3.2.2 as uniform score inflation.

### Why C++ reorders

The C++ rank instability has a documented cause rather than being unexplained. `TODO.md` records
that GPT-5.6 Terra high and max C++ "both implemented the documented M2/M30-or-percent
requirement" and were heavily penalized by the undelimited v3.2.0 fixtures. Under the framed
v3.2.2 fixtures they move 47.80% → 83.06% and 48.72% → 98.20%. Luna-high moves the other way for
the opposite reason. Both directions trace to percent handling, which the old fixtures could not
observe.

Historical 3.2.0 orderings are therefore not a reliable proxy for ordering under the corrected
rubric. They remain accurate descriptions of performance under the historical rubric.

## Scorer-completion audit

Before the v3.2.2 work, `run_hidden_tests` fell through to `parse_json_report` after both Docker
attempts failed, so a partial or stale report could in principle have been scored as a real result.
That path now raises. All 1,776 published run files across every eval were checked: every `tests`
array length equals `test_summary.total`, outcome components sum to the total, per-outcome counts
reconcile, and every `(task, eval_version)` pair has exactly one distinct total.

**No count inconsistencies were detected.** That is the whole of the claim. These checks do not
establish that every historical grading run completed, for three reasons: `parse_json_report`
builds both the outcome list and the summary from the same `tests` array, so their agreement is
structural rather than independent; agreement among peer totals is not comparison against an
independently known intended node set, and cannot detect the same partial subset across peers, an
equal-sized wrong or duplicated node set, or a stale but complete report; and a complete-looking
report does not by itself establish that the surrounding run succeeded. Establishing completion
would require retained reports and logs, exit status, and expected node identities per suite.
Absence of detected anomalies is not proof of absence.

## Limits

- The two cohorts were graded under **different Docker images** — Astra under `sha256:9a4f1fe0…`,
  this cohort under `sha256:922c3bc7…`, the former being unavailable on the auditing workstation.
  Cross-cohort comparison is indicative, not a shared-grader result.
- This is a cross-cohort check, **not a blinded holdout**; see the TODO disclosure above.
- The twelve Astra submissions were **not** rerun; their preserved source is not on this machine.
  Verification of that cohort is of the committed records, not of a re-execution.
- The grading-machine mutation controls were not reproduced, beyond one independently
  reconstructed persisted-value corruption mutant that failed its four intended cases as claimed.
- The **Rust reference suite** was not validated in this environment: it depends on `serde` and
  `serde_json`, which cannot be fetched during an offline build without a pre-populated cargo
  cache. This is dependency-specific and not a missing compiler. Rust submissions built and graded
  normally — `RustBackend.prepare` compiles fresh into a newly allocated build directory and does
  not reuse any `target/` tree in the saved source, and a dependency-free submission compiles
  offline without difficulty. Rust terra-low's zero is a faithful reproduction of a submission that
  did not build in its original run either.
- The negative-test decoupling in `run_rs274_invalid_input` is a real reweighting: roughly 50 cases
  now require exit 1 plus nonempty JSON, with four dedicated schema cases owning the error field.
  Growth from 546 to 555 collected tests is a statement about test inventory, not proof that
  coverage is unchanged.
- One saved run per language and effort. Nothing here supports population-level model or
  reasoning-effort rankings.

## Conclusions

The independent checks support the exercised v3.2.2 fixture repairs, and the regrades demonstrate
benefits beyond Astra: saved submissions from a different model family gain under the corrected
rubric as that cohort did. This does not establish independence from prior design influence.

A ceiling concern applies to the strongest saved submissions: all four GPT-5.6 sol-max rows reach
554–555 of 555, alongside Astra's 549–555. It is not a blanket saturation claim — at low reasoning
effort the cohorts separate clearly, with GPT-5.6 sol-low at 390–485 against Astra low at 549–554.
Comparisons among the top submissions are measuring the ceiling; comparisons across the full range
are not.

Promoting any regraded cohort into the published record must move every cohort intended for
comparison; see the cohort rules in [`docs/operations/Regrading.md`](../operations/Regrading.md).
The published RS274 rows already span two grading versions (339 at 3.1.11, 48 at 3.2.0), so the
published table should not be assumed internally consistent today.
